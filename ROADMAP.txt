THE CATALOGUE -- ROADMAP
========================

What is deliberately not built yet, and why. Everything here was considered and
parked on purpose -- none of it is rejected. Landed work lives in CHANGELOG.md;
this file is the other half of that story.

Current version: 0.7.1-beta. 1.0.0 is reserved for the first build that has been
played end to end. It does NOT promise a dedicated server: server authority is
deferred past 1.0 rather than half-done before it, and the README says the same.

The number went DOWN at the alpha/beta line, on purpose: the alpha line ended at
0.13.0 and the beta line starts its own count at 0.1.0. Nothing has been released,
so no install anywhere reads it as a downgrade. See the CHANGELOG entry.

WHAT MOVED THIS TO BETA. Named by Vitor on 2026-08-29: the alpha ends when the work
turns to how the thing LOOKS and how far it REACHES, rather than what it does. The
banking feature in 0.1.0-beta is the reach half of that -- the mod stopped being a
book in a bag and acquired a place in the world you have to walk to.

The look half has NOT started and is still owed: textured window art in the manner
of Knox Chronicles, and the hand-made icons and models for the catalogue and the
parcels (item 9 below). The first real look at multiplayer (item 2) is still after
1.0 and is unaffected by the numbering.


BEFORE 1.0
----------

1. Workshop packaging

   What the mod still lacks is the Steam Workshop wrapper: a workshop.txt at the
   upload root naming the item, its tags and its visibility, and a preview image
   beside it. Do not confuse that preview with 42/preview.png, which already
   exists -- that one is the poster mod.info points at, drawn by the game's own
   mod browser. The Workshop needs its own, at the root, outside 42/.

   (The repo itself is already public, so that part is done.)


AFTER 1.0
---------

2. Multiplayer / server authority

   Every transaction runs client-side, so on a dedicated server the mod is
   trivially cheatable. Prices, money deduction and order state all have to move
   behind server commands together, which is why it is one job and not a series
   of small ones. Until then the mod is documented as single-player only.


CONTENT AND ECONOMY
-------------------

3. Limited stock and scarcity

   Stock is infinite. Finite stock with restocking would give buying a real sense
   of scarcity, but it needs stock levels that survive a save -- and PZ has no
   save hook, so the state has to live on player:getModData() like the ledger
   does.

4. Shipping cost and named delivery tiers

   Delivery today is one speed plus Rush, and Rush is the only thing that costs
   extra (RushFeePercent, 20%). The parked idea is a real freight cost by weight
   and bulk, with Economy / Standard / Express as named choices at checkout, so
   that ordering an engine block is not priced like ordering a screwdriver.

5. Used and clearance goods

   Occasional discounted offers at a predefined condition, so the catalogue is
   not always mint-condition stock. The condition-scaling code the sell window
   already uses would do most of the work.

6. Dynamic economy

   Prices and availability drifting with weeks survived: medicine and ammunition
   climb, decoration falls. Cheap to fake with a multiplier over TC_PriceTable,
   expensive to make feel deliberate rather than random.

6a. Paying for an order from the account

   Banking landed in 0.1.0-beta and the two halves do not touch yet: an order is
   still settled out of the notes in your pockets, and the account is only a place
   to leave them. Named by Vitor as the point of the feature, so this is a WHEN and
   not an IF.

   It is parked rather than done because it is a design question and not a wiring
   one. If the catalogue can charge the account from anywhere, cash stops mattering
   and the walk to the machine was for nothing; if it can only charge it while you
   are standing at a machine, the catalogue becomes a thing you use at ATMs.

   The third reading -- the card is what the catalogue bills, and having it on you
   is the requirement -- is the one worth trying first, and 0.2.0-beta has already
   built the half it needs: the card is the account, it has to be on your person,
   and losing it costs the balance. What is left is deciding whether the catalogue
   may read it at all, and TC.cardsOnPlayer is the function that would answer.

7. Catalogue inserts

   Flavour pages bound into the catalogue -- adverts, notices, a company voice.
   Pure presentation, no mechanics.


PRESENTATION
------------

8. PT-BR translation

   English only for now. Cheap to revisit: the JSON translation format and the
   key-prefix-decides-the-file rule are both written down in CLAUDE.md, which is
   what made this expensive the first time.

9. Hand-made parcel models

   The three delivery tiers share vanilla's Base.Parcel_ExtraLarge mesh and
   differ only by icon. Vitor is modelling the oversized parcels by hand; the
   script definitions in 42/media/scripts/thecatalogue.txt are ready for them.


STRUCK OFF -- ALREADY SHIPPED
-----------------------------

Kept here so nothing on the list gets proposed twice:

  - Orders with a delivery wait, and physical delivery to a container
  - Rush delivery: skip the wait for a surcharge over the order total
  - Cancelling an order, and refusing it at the door
  - The recipe that bundles 100 loose notes
  - Affordable / owned / not-owned filters, and the per-character wishlist
  - B42 fluid containers handled in the condition scaling
  - Requiring the catalogue in hand while the window is open
  - Mod-item integration: other mods' items priced by the same rules and
    filterable by source mod
  - Cash machines: one account per credit card, a PIN, deposits and
    withdrawals in real notes at any vanilla ATM, and transfers between
    accounts addressed by the last four digits. The card must be on you.
